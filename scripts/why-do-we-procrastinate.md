**[TITLE]**

Why Do We Procrastinate?

**[SCRIPT]**

Here's the surprising truth: procrastination isn't a time-management problem. It's an emotion-management problem.

When a task makes us feel bored, anxious, or afraid of failing, our brain treats that discomfort like a threat. The amygdala, your brain's alarm system, sounds off, and you reach for relief. A quick scroll. A snack. "Just five minutes." And it works... for a moment.

That instant relief rewards the avoidance, so your brain learns to do it again. Meanwhile, the prefrontal cortex, the part that plans ahead, loses the tug-of-war. Researchers have even found that we picture our future selves almost like strangers. So we hand them the stress.

The good news? You can break the loop. Shrink the task until it feels almost silly: open the document, write one sentence, put on your shoes. Forgive yourself for past delays; studies show self-compassion reduces procrastination. And remember: motivation often follows action, not the other way around.

So... what's the one thing you've been putting off? Start it. Right now. Just for two minutes.
